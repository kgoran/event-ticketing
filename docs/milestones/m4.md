## Goal

The modules' events travel over Kafka: ordered per order, event or ticket, delivered at least
once and handled once, with poison messages set aside instead of blocking the rest, and a new
consumer able to read what happened before it existed. On that, money at volume: an organizer
cancels an event and every order is refunded at the provider's rate limit, none twice (T9);
tickets are issued within T6 when a burst of payments arrives; a chargeback revokes the order's
tickets at the door. Reporting builds sales and check-in figures from the stream. Workers run as
separate deployments by configuration, and the Scaling Lab shows their backlog.

## Decisions

Decided overnight with the recommended answer; each one waits for the owner's review.

| # | Decision | Why | Alternatives considered |
|---|---|---|---|
| D76 | **Redpanda, three brokers in the lab, one per zone**: replication factor 3, `acks=all`, at least 2 in sync; one broker under Aspire; Redpanda Console in both. Each broker capped at about 1 GB | ADR-0003's choice. Three brokers teach leaders, in-sync replicas and a broker lost with its zone (the zone drill) | One broker in the lab (no replication to see; zone-a would have to hold it); Apache Kafka in KRaft mode (heavier JVM brokers for the same protocol) |
| D77 | **Confluent.Kafka** (librdkafka), configured by Aspire's integration where it helps; its health checks off (D74) | The standard .NET client; Aspire wires its configuration and telemetry | KafkaFlow, Silverback (frameworks over the same client: more to learn than to gain); MassTransit's Kafka rider (commercial since v9) |
| D78 | **A topic per publishing module** (`eventticketing.orders`, …), **keyed by the entity whose order matters** (order ID for orders and payments, event ID for catalog, ticket's order for ticketing). 12 partitions each, retained 7 days, zstd | Per-entity ordering across a module's event types (an order's Confirmed before its Refunded), parallelism by partition; ADR-0014: partitions generous up front | A topic per event type (an order's events on different topics lose their order); one topic for everything (every consumer reads everything) |
| D79 | **Protobuf, code-first** with protobuf-net: the contracts stay C# records, each property with an explicit field number; `.proto` files generated from them and committed next to each contracts project, and a test fails when they are stale (as the OpenAPI documents). Instants as `google.protobuf.Timestamp`, IDs as strings | ADR-0003 chose Protobuf for its rules of evolution: field numbers, never reused. Code-first keeps the records every handler and test uses | `.proto` first with Google.Protobuf (generated mutable classes replace the records; every handler and test changes); JSON (ADR-0003 chose Protobuf; JSON hides evolution mistakes until a consumer fails) |
| D80 | **Big messages stay whole:** `EventPublished` carries every seat (30,000 for the load-test venue: about 1.4 MB). The catalog topic and the clients allow 16 MB | A message is the unit of ordering and of the inbox; splitting one event into parts makes consumers reassemble | The claim check (seats in SeaweedFS, a reference in the message: right for unbounded payloads, more moving parts for a bounded one); Kafka's 1 MB default (fails at 20,000 seats) |
| D81 | **One consumer group per consuming module** (`eventticketing.ticketing`, …). A partition's messages are handled in order, each through the inbox as today; the offset is committed after the inbox row. A failing message is retried in place with backoff for about a minute (a failover passes), then goes to the group's dead-letter topic with the error, and the partition moves on. `lab dlq` lists and replays | At-least-once delivery plus the inbox is handled once; a minute rides out a failover; a poison message must not stop an event's other messages forever | Retry topics with delays (more topics, more ordering surprises); blocking the partition until fixed (one bad message stops every order on it) |
| D82 | **The transport by configuration:** Kafka under Aspire and in the lab; in-process (M1–M3's) in the product's tests and wherever no broker is configured | Tests stay fast and need no broker; the system tests and the lab run the real thing | Kafka in every test (slower, flaky in CI); two code paths chosen at compile time |
| D83 | **Workers split by configuration:** a deployment lists the modules whose consumers it runs, and whether it runs the relay and the scheduler. The lab runs `workers` (relay, scheduler, every module but Notifications) ×2 and `workers-notifications` ×1 | ADR-0014's "one module can scale separately without becoming a service"; email is slow and bursty | A deployment per module (eight deployments for the lab's memory); no split until M5 |
| D84 | **Reporting** as a module of its own: projections in its schema from Orders', Payments', Ticketing's and CheckIn's events (sold, revenue, refunded, revoked, checked in, per event and hour); BackOfficeApi `GET /events/{id}/report`. A new group starts from the earliest offset, so it builds history by replay | The brief's analytics read model; the cure for M3's lesson 10 (a module added later misses what happened before it) | Queries across modules' schemas (ADR-0002 forbids); a separate analytics database (M6+) |
| D85 | **Event cancellation as §4.7 has it:** Catalog `POST /events/{id}/cancellation` (the event's organizer or an admin) publishes `EventCancelled`. Inventory stops holds; Ticketing revokes every ticket in pages of 500; Orders runs one cancellation per event, which cancels open checkouts and moves confirmed orders to Refunding in pages; Notifications emails each buyer; BackOfficeApi shows the progress | Each module reacts on its own, plus one tracker for "is it done"; pages keep transactions and messages small | One coordinator calling every module (checkout's pattern: right for one order, wrong for 50,000) |
| D86 | **Refunds at the provider's rate:** Payments takes a token per provider call from a limiter in Valkey shared by every Workers instance (LocalPay's limit configurable, Stripe's 25/s); a 429 from the provider waits its Retry-After. Without Valkey, each instance takes its share of the limit. LocalPay gets a configurable rate limit | A rate limit is the provider's, across all our instances; 429s are the safety net, not the plan | Each instance at the full limit (N× over it); one refund worker instance (no failover) |
| D87 | **T9's mechanics at lab scale in M4** (about 1,500 orders, the lab's fans; LocalPay at 50 refunds/s): every order final, no payment refunded twice, the time close to orders ÷ limit. **The full 50,000 in M5**, with the load-test data set and venue (ADR-0015) | 200 lab fans and the 8-ticket limit can't make 50,000 orders through the API; M5 builds the load-test data set for T1 and T3 anyway | A data set inserted with SQL now (brittle across five modules' schemas, before M5 needs it); thousands of Keycloak users |
| D88 | **Chargebacks:** LocalPay `POST /payments/{id}/dispute` sends a dispute webhook (Stripe's `charge.dispute.created` maps the same). Payments records it and publishes `DisputeOpened`; Orders revokes the order (`OrderRevoked`), Ticketing its tickets, which CheckIn's door lists refuse. No refund: the provider took the money back | Architecture §4.7: the same path as a revocation | Treating a dispute as a refund (we would pay twice) |
| D89 | **T6 measured:** `OrderConfirmed` and `TicketsIssued` gain `PaidAt` (a new field, evolution in practice); histograms from payment to tickets issued and to email accepted. A payment burst is LocalPay holding webhooks and sending them together. The Scaling Lab's **Workers** panel: lag per consumer group, the outbox's unsent rows per module, dead letters; the *Payment burst* preset | T6's definition; a burst is what fills the backlog the panel shows | Measuring from logs (no histogram); a burst of real checkouts (the hold path would be the bottleneck, not the Workers) |
| D90 | **The M4 demo** (runbook `docs/demos/m4.md`) and its dry run in the nightly lab workflow | As M2 and M3 | — |

## Definition of done

1. The modules' events travel over Kafka in the lab and under Aspire; the system tests pass on it.
2. A broker lost with its zone loses no event; a consumer crash redelivers, and the inbox drops
   the duplicates.
3. A poison message is set aside, the rest of its partition goes on, and it can be replayed.
4. Reporting, deployed after the events happened, shows them.
5. An event's cancellation refunds every order at the provider's rate, none twice (T9's mechanics).
6. A chargeback revokes the order's tickets at the door.
7. T6 holds in a payment burst; the Scaling Lab shows the Workers' backlog fill and drain.
8. Every new decision has an ADR.

## Demo (about 30 minutes)

1. Redpanda Console: the topics, their partitions and leaders; an order's events on one partition.
2. Kill a broker (its zone): producing and consuming go on; the partitions' leaders move.
3. A poison message: the dead-letter topic, the partition moving on, `lab dlq replay`.
4. Reporting deployed after the sales: its figures appear by replay.
5. The payment burst: the Workers panel's backlog fills and drains; T6's histogram.
6. Cancel the demo event: checkouts cancelled, tickets refused at the door, refunds at LocalPay's
   rate, the progress endpoint reaching 100%; T2's checker.
7. A chargeback: the fan's tickets refused at the scanner.

## Risks

- **Memory.** Three brokers cost about 2.5–3 GB; the lab used about 8.4 GB of Docker's 15 GB
  after M3. If it doesn't fit on a 16 GB laptop, one broker (D76's alternative) is the fallback.
- **Ordering assumptions.** M1–M3 promised no order; consumers were written for that. Kafka gives
  order per key only: a consumer that now assumes order across topics would be wrong.
- **Rebalances.** Workers scaling or rolling restarts stop a group's consumption for a moment;
  cooperative rebalancing and static membership keep it short. Measured in KF1.
- **Large messages.** D80's 16 MB holds for the load-test venue; a bigger venue needs the claim
  check.
